# QUIZ — Asset Management

This page contains the Q & A (questions and answers) for this chapter — Chapter 25: Asset Management. Work through
these after reading the chapter, while the material is fresh — recall practice is what cements
new knowledge into long-term memory.

Try every question before you look below. Each one carries a clue, so nothing here should
leave you stuck. The answers are all together in the Answers section further down, numbered

This chapter is a short one, so its quiz is short too.


## QUESTIONS

1) What do asset management tools actually do for a website? Name two of the jobs the chapter
   mentions.

   Clue: the chapter lists three. One turns many files into fewer, one makes files smaller, and
   one helps the browser notice when you have published a newer version.


2) The chapter names three asset management tools. Name any two of them.

   Clue: they are build tools, and the chapter says they are mostly used on big projects,
   especially alongside frameworks like React and Vue.


3) Why has this book not asked you to use any of these tools?

   Clue: think about the size of the projects you have built here, and about what you learn by
   linking your HTML, CSS and JavaScript files together yourself.


## ANSWERS

1) Any two of these three:

   - They **combine several files into one**, so the browser makes fewer requests and the site
     loads faster.
   - They **shrink file sizes**, which saves both space and loading time.
   - They **help the browser pick up the newest version** of your files, rather than serving an
     old copy it has remembered.

   All three are really the same idea wearing different hats: getting the right files to your
   visitor as quickly as possible.


2) Any two of **Webpack**, **Vite** and **Parcel**.

   You do not need to know how to use them yet. It is enough to recognise the names, so that when
   you meet them in a job advert or someone else's project you know what kind of thing they are.


3) Because everything you have built in this book has been small and simple — a handful of files
   you can organise into folders yourself.

   Linking your own files together by hand is not a limitation, it is part of the training. It
   teaches you what a page is actually loading and in what order, which is exactly the knowledge
   these tools hide from you once you start using them. When you later move on to larger apps or
   frameworks, that is the point at which asset management starts to earn its keep — and by then
   you will have the background to pick it up with confidence.
